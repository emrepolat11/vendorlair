A practical contract renewal tracking checklist

To track a vendor contract renewal, record the expiry date, confirm the notice terms and set an internal decision date before notice is due. Assign an owner and keep the decision with the supplier record.

Expiry date is not the decision deadline

An agreement can require notice well before its expiry date. Some renew automatically. The exact contract wording determines how notice is calculated and delivered, so use the signed agreement as your source.

A worked example

Suppose a contract expires on 31 December 2026 and requires 60 calendar days’ notice. Simple date subtraction gives 1 November 2026. If you reserve another 30 days for internal review, your planning date is 2 October 2026. Check the contract’s counting rules, delivery requirements and business-day provisions before relying on either date.

Before the review

  • Find the latest signed agreement and any amendments.
  • Record expiry, automatic renewal and notice terms.
  • Assign the internal owner and decision approver.
  • Collect current costs, usage and unresolved service issues.

During the review

  • Confirm whether the business still needs the service.
  • Ask the supplier about pricing or scope changes.
  • Allow time for alternatives if switching is possible.
  • Record whether to renew, renegotiate or end the agreement.

After the decision

  • Send any required notice using the method specified in the agreement.
  • Keep a record of the notice and acknowledgement.
  • Update the next contract dates, owner and follow-up actions.
